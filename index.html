Instruments that behave like instruments.

Every tool a technician reaches for, with the same quirks and the same limits.

Multimeter

A 6000-count meter, not an ideal probe: 10 MΩ input, contact resistance, diode test. Capacitance reads high in circuit. The 10 A input only works in series.

Bench supply

Boot current you can read: standby, sleep, running. Current limit, and voltage injection to make the culprit on a shorted rail heat up.

Oscilloscope

Roll through power-up ramps and boot loops. Catch a switch node, its ripple, or a clock that never started.

Thermal camera

IR that reads like IR: bare solder looks cold. Freeze spray, isopropyl and the rosin method to pin the hot part.

Rework

Iron, hot air, preheater. Flux matters, ground pads fight back, and pads lift if you cook them.

Boardview

Both sides, 2D or a full 3D orbit. Click a part to see where its nets go. Search hundreds of parts in a keystroke.

Boards fail the way real boards fail.

Not "part missing, find it". Faults come with realistic severity and realistic symptoms. Some only show up when the board is warm, or when you press on it.

  • dead-short MLCC
  • leaky capacitor
  • liquid residue
  • corroded copper
  • chip shorted inside
  • dead regulator
  • drifted resistor
  • resistive cold joint
  • opens when warm
  • works only when pressed
  • leaks when warm
  • lifted pad
  • cracked by thermal shock
  • reversed part

Any board. No per-board scripting.

Import a boardview (.brd, .tvw) or a KiCad layout. DEADBOARD finds the power tree, the regulators and the power states from the netlist and its net names, then simulates the whole board with a SPICE engine. Export it as an ngspice netlist whenever you like.

Made something? Package your boards, their BOM and schematic as a mod that anyone can install.

Every number has a source.

Values come from the board data, datasheets and design intent. Where none exist, the estimate is labelled as an estimate, and synthesized waveforms say so on the scope. Measured a real board? Feed the readings back and DEADBOARD calibrates its model against your bench.

Break it. Lock a flag. Share the link.

Start from a working board, plant faults, and lock a flag to the repaired circuit. The flag is encrypted with the board's topology, so only a real repair unlocks it. There's no peeking and no shortcut. Share it as a file or a single link.
